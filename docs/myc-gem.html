﻿# Two Solana micro-caps with products people can actually use: $SQUIRE and $AGENC

AI-created for a @myceliumfinance bounty. Market data snapshot: 2026-10-24 22:47 UTC.

The Mycelium Strategy connects selected Solana projects into liquidity pools — the brief we worked from says more than 84% of tradable $MYC already sits in pools, and trading fees fund the next connections. So the interesting question is not "which token pumps", but "what could we plant next?".

We took the sub-$1M market-cap corner of Solana seriously: 110 tokens passed the numeric filters out of 4,040 Jupiter-verified tokens, and we then checked products, activity, holder concentration and liquidity by hand. Two projects survived. Most of the rest were memes, ticker clones (a wall of "Backpack Securities" AAPL/NVDA look-alikes) or bots with no product.

We deliberately selected two of the three allowed slots. A third pick would have meant lowering the evidence bar, and the brief says quality matters more than filling the list.

Why $SQUIRE stands out

$SQUIRE is the token of Sortis AI's agent-first tooling family. The product layer is real and public today: cli.city lists eight open-source command-line tools built by AI agents, usepod.ai is a live inference marketplace that routes OpenAI- and Anthropic-compatible requests to the cheapest provider and settles per request in USDC over x402, and agent-dns.org sells DNS management per request for autonomous agents — no accounts, no API keys. Pod the Trader, an agent trading bot, is the consumer showcase.

Evidence of a working product, not a roadmap: the UsePod documentation repository was pushed on 2026-10-21, three days before this snapshot, and the marketplace site advertises live providers and per-request x402 billing. The $SQUIRE pair has traded since 2026-03-18 (219 days), which is long enough that the "$1M cap" is not a launch-week artifact.

The honest problem is the token itself: the only documented "utility" is "Support us by holding $SQUIRE". No fee share, no staking, no buyback is described on any project page we read. That makes $SQUIRE a bet on the team's product roadmap rather than on a token with a claim on revenue — and the $21k daily volume means a MYC pair would be thin.

Why $AGENC stands out

AgenC is the protocol and SDK for launching agent marketplaces: a requester escrows SOL on-chain for a task, an AI agent claims and delivers it, and settlement (accept, reject, cancel) is enforced by a Solana program with on-chain receipts. agenc.ag is the live reference marketplace — the homepage streams real settlement instructions with values, and the docs describe competitive and creator-review task modes.

Activity here is denser than at most micro-caps: AgenC Core shipped 0.13 → 0.14 → 0.19.13 between October 13 and October 18, the team published a Terminal-Bench 2.1 result (76.2% for $34 with DeepSeek V4.1 Flash) on 2026-10-16, opened AgenC Studios on 2026-10-18, and agenc-core was pushed to GitHub on 2026-10-24 — the same day as this snapshot.

The risk is concentration. After excluding the pump.fun AMM pool, the top ten accounts hold 54.3% of supply: one unidentified wallet holds 38.15%, and another 7.29% sits in a Token-2022 account whose last four transactions ran on the 26th of June, July, August and September — the footprint of a monthly vesting claim. Rugcheck scores it 37/100 and flags "single holder ownership". As with $SQUIRE, $AGENC's utility is not documented: nothing on the site or in the docs ties the token to protocol fees.

Selection criteria and evidence

  • On Solana — both mints are SPL tokens on mainnet.
  • Circulating market cap below $1M — $742.8k and $823.6k (DexScreener, 2026-10-24 22:47 UTC).
  • Working product usable today — usepod.ai / cli.city / agent-dns.org; agenc.ag mainnet marketplace.
  • Not a meme token — both ship software with public repos and docs.
  • Trading for at least 60 days — 219 and 241 days.
  • ≥ $10,000 24h volume — $21,009 and $98,278.
  • ≥ $50,000 DEX liquidity, no double counting — $124,620 and $133,699 in a single main pool each.
  • Product update or team communication within 30 days — usepod-docs pushed 2026-10-21; agenc-core pushed 2026-10-24, Core 0.19.13 on 2026-10-18.
  • Not already in the Mycelium Strategy — checked against the live allocation feed on 2026-10-24 22:44 UTC: KNOTS, PRCL, GABLE, SOL, KINS, DREAMS, GUAC, STREAM, JUBJUB, USDC. Neither token appears.
  • Not SOL/USDC/USDT/MYC/ESF — neither is.

Candidates we rejected on evidence

$CHATOSHI (mcap $571k, liquidity $143k, volume $24k) looks attractive on numbers, and Chatoshi really does ship a Solana Web3 browser with desktop, mobile and extension downloads. But we found no product update in the last 30 days — the public sitemap's last modification is 2026-04-20 and no repository activity surfaced — and only about 56% of the main Raydium liquidity is locked. That fails the "substantive update within 30 days" filter on the evidence available to us.

$THREE (three.ws, 3D agent layer) sits on the volume floor: $10,039 at our snapshot and $9,218 earlier the same evening, against a $10,000 minimum. It is worth another look later, not today.

How each could fit the Strategy

For $SQUIRE the realistic roles are collaboration and holding. A collaboration is concrete: use the UsePod marketplace and sell paid inference or DNS services through Mycelium's x402 tooling, so both ecosystems get real agent traffic. A MYC/SQUIRE pair is the weaker option — $21k of daily volume cannot support a pool that earns its keep.

For $AGENC the same logic applies with more substance: the protocol already pays agents on-chain, so a collaboration (Mycelium posting paid research tasks, or AgenC marketplaces settling in USDC on Base/Solana) has product-market fit before any token thesis. A holding case exists only if the team later routes protocol fees to $AGENC; today that link is undocumented, so we would not size it as a fee-earning asset.

Neither project has partnered with Mycelium, and nothing here is an allocation recommendation.
